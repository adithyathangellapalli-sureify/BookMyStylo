 
 Renaming Snipbook 
 Naming board · Snipbook rebrand
 Seven names, one decision,
four marks to choose from.
 Snipbook works, but it spends its whole name on scissors — and the product is
 already bigger than that: a locality-first salon booking network where a customer picks a
 chair and a slot , and a shop runs its day from the partner app. Seven
 candidates below, each buying something different with the rename — then the four mark
 directions for the name that won.
 Snipbook 
 Retiring 
 What the name has to carry
 Two apps, one family. A customer app and a partner app for shop owners, off one
 wordmark.
 Locality is the product. Discovery is by city and neighbourhood, not by brand.
 Chair + slot is the unit. Not "appointment" — a specific chair, held for minutes.
 Not barbers only. Unisex salons, threading, colour, kids' cuts.
 Constraints I designed against
 Sayable over a phone call in Hindi or English, first try, no spelling it out.
 Nine characters or fewer — it sits in a 60px app bar next to a back arrow.
 One mark, three sizes: 20px favicon, app icon, shop signage sticker.
 No caste-linked words. "Nai" and its variants are off the table for this reason.
 Where this landed
 BookMyStylo 
 This is the pick — four mark
 directions for it are further down the page. It keeps the stylist-first promise and fixes
 the appliance problem "Styler" had. Two things still stand from the naming work: my own
 recommendation was Chairly , and the case for it is still in the
 first row; and the BookMy prefix is the one piece I'd still get
 cleared by a lawyer. So every direction below is drawn to work with the prefix deleted — if
 you ever ship as plain Stylo , no mark gets redrawn.
 Chairly 
 Chairly for Salons 
 20px · one colour 
 Recommended 
 The inventory word 
 Chair + -ly. The chair is the bookable unit in your schema — chairs,
 holds, slots. The name says the model out loud.
 This is the only candidate that describes what a customer receives without
 naming a service, so it never boxes you in: today it's a fade, next year it's a keratin
 treatment or a nail station, and the name doesn't strain. It reads as a platform to shop
 owners — which is who you need to sign — and the mark is a barber chair in profile that still
 resolves at 20px, because it's four rectangles.
 Book the chair, not the queue.

 Chivo 600 , tracking −0.028em. Squared terminals sit flush against the mark.

 Chair in profile — backrest, seat, pedestal, base, at 55%/38% opacity for depth.
 Palette
 Keeps your teal. #1c8a79 is a half-step deeper than the shipped #0d9488 — no reskin needed.

 and reads faintly SaaS. Check for furniture and co-working use of "Chairly" before you file.
 Kesh 
 Kesh Studio 
 20px · thin curve drops out — use the heavy cut 
 Alternative — premium 
 The Sanskrit root 
 केश — hair. Sanskrit, and legible across Hindi, Marathi, Gujarati and
 Bengali. Four letters, one syllable, no spelling required on a phone call.
 This is the most grown-up name on the board and the only one that reads
 salon rather than barbershop — which matters, because women's salons carry
 higher ticket sizes and are the harder side of the marketplace to win. Set in Bodoni with
 wide tracking it looks like a brand a good salon would put on its own glass door, which is
 exactly the co-branding you want from partners.

 Bodoni Moda 500 , uppercase, tracking +0.11em. High-contrast strokes read as beauty-editorial.
 Mark
 A lock of hair as two nested curves — the second at 42% is decoration and drops at small sizes.
 Palette
 New palette. Indigo #5a46c8 on porcelain; the teal would fight the didone.
 Partner app
 Kesh Studio — the shop-side tool, named like equipment.
 Read this before you commit: kesh is also one of the Five
 Ks in Sikhism — uncut hair kept as a religious article of faith. A haircut-booking brand
 named Kesh can land badly with Sikh customers and partners, particularly in Punjab and
 Delhi. It is not a blocker everywhere, but it is a real reputational risk that a founder
 should decide on deliberately rather than discover in a review. Keshya or
 Keshi soften the direct reference if you like the sound but not the exposure.

 The locality play 
 Gully + cut. Gully is the lane your shop is actually on — the
 unit of geography people navigate by, and the one your discovery flow is built around.

 Styler 
 Book my Styler for Salons 
 20px · mark survives, the name can't come with it 
 The repair — same idea, ownable 
 Stylo 
 Your suggestion — not recommended as written 
 The stylist-first play 
 Book + my + styler. A whole sentence as a name, in the
 BookMyShow / MakeMyTrip grammar Indian users already know.
 The instinct behind it is the best one on this
 board. Every other name here books a chair ; this one books a person — and
 you have already shipped the feature that makes that true: stylist profiles, the stylist
 picker, customer reviews. Stylist-first is a genuinely stronger promise than chair-first,
 because a returning customer wants Imran on Saturday, not seat two. StyleSeat built a US
 business on exactly that claim. So the strategy is right. It's the three words that don't
 survive contact.
 Book my Styler
 Imran has 3 slots open on Saturday. You've booked him twice before.
 Wordmark
 Chivo 900 under a mono kicker — stacking "BOOK MY" above "Styler" is the only way a three-word name holds together as a lockup.
 Mark
 A stylist inside a bookmark: head and shoulders over a ribbon that notches into a slot. Legible at 20px.
 Palette
 New palette. Deliberate blue #1f6fb2 — distance from BookMyShow's red is a legal precaution, not a taste one.
 Partner app
 Book my Styler for Salons — now five words on a login screen.
 Three problems, in order of seriousness. 
 One — the "BookMy" prefix is owned territory in India. BookMyShow is the dominant
 mark in that formation and its owner has a track record of challenging
 BookMy… names; a lawyer's clearance opinion here is not optional, and the answer may
 well be no.
 Two — a "styler" is an appliance, not a person. The person is a
 stylist ; "styler" is how Dyson and Philips label hot-air tools, so you would compete
 for that phrase against appliance shopping in every search and ad auction.
 Three — it is fourteen characters and three words. A home-screen icon label truncates
 around eleven, so it ships as "BookMySty…" on real phones, and your 60px app bar can't hold
 it beside a back arrow.
 Keep the strategy, change the string
 Stylo — five characters, stylist-first, and it already means "stylish" in everyday
 Indian English, so it carries the aspiration for free. Same mark, same promise, none of the
 three problems. Styla and Stylr are the same move if Stylo is taken; check
 footwear and stationery classes, where "Stylo" has prior use.
 If you'd rather keep the sentence, BookMyStylist at least fixes the grammar — but it
 makes the trademark exposure worse, not better, and adds a character.

 One honest note on the incumbent: Snipbook
 is not a bad name. If the rename doesn't buy you something specific — room to grow past
 barbering, or recall in the neighbourhoods you're actually launching in — the cheapest option on
 this board is keeping it.
 The chosen name
 Four ways to draw BookMyStylo.
 Why the first mark missed: it was two stock glyphs stacked — a default avatar inside a
 default bookmark. Nothing in it came from hair, nothing came from your booking mechanic, and it
 left the actual brand word out of the picture. Fixed below: every direction takes its shape from
 either a salon object or the slot model, and two of them put the mark inside the word.
 Two constraints these have to solve. Eleven characters over three words means the
 lockup has to be stacked or weight-contrasted — one flat line of BookMyStylo at icon
 size is unreadable. And there is a monogram trap: BMS is already how people abbreviate
 BookMyShow, and BS is worse, so the only safe short form is a single S .
 If you want one answer: Direction A. It's the only one where the mark is a
 letter of the name, which means the icon, the favicon and the wordmark are the same object —
 and it survives the prefix being dropped.

 Direction A — recommended 
 The mirror 
 The O becomes the mirror. Every salon chair in the world faces one,
 and no competitor owns it — the category is drowning in scissors.
 This is the structural idea the first attempt was missing: the mark isn't
 next to the name, it's a letter of it, so the app icon, the favicon and
 the wordmark are all the same object. The highlight arc is the only decorative stroke and
 it's the first thing to drop at small sizes. Brass on slate reads salon-premium rather than
 marketplace-cheap, which matters when the mark has to sit on a partner shop's own glass
 door next to their name.
 Every good chair faces one.
 Imran's chair is free at 6:30. Two seats from the window.
 Wordmark
 Bodoni Moda 600 , caps, tracking +0.1em, under a mono "Book my" kicker at 0.22em.
 Mark
 Mirror on a stand — a stroked ring, one highlight arc, one stem. Three shapes.
 Palette
 Brass #a9781f on the cool slate ground. Warm metal, not tech-teal.
 Drop the prefix
 Works untouched — "STYL◯" is already the whole logo.
 Watch out: a stroked ring is the most common shape in software, so the
 mirror only reads as a mirror because of the stand and the highlight — don't let a
 developer simplify those away. Bodoni's thin strokes also need a minimum size; below 14px
 the wordmark needs the heavier optical cut.

 Direction B 
 The comb 
 The one salon tool that isn't scissors. Instantly readable, tilted
 fourteen degrees so it looks in-use rather than in a display case.
 The most legible mark of the four and the easiest to hand to a signage
 printer — four teeth and a spine, no strokes, no gradients, no opacity tricks. It also lets

 prefix, Stylo at 900 becomes the brand, and you can literally delete the light

 Once you pick
 Two things happen next.
 Clear the name first
 I can't check any of these from here — no network access in this session — so
 treat BookMyStylo as unverified until you've run this list.
 For this name the first item is the gating question rather than a formality: the
 BookMy prefix is the exposure, and the answer decides whether you ship the prefix or
 just Stylo .
 Trademark, class 44 (beauty salon
 services) and class 42/9 (software) on the IP India register. 
 Company name availability on the
 MCA portal, if the brand becomes the entity. 
 Domain — the .in 
 matters more than the .com for a locality product. 
 Play Store and App Store name
 search — a live app with the name is a harder blocker than a domain.
